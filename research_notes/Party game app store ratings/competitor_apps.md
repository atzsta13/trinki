# Party / drinking card game apps: store ratings, self-presentation, content gating (competitor scan, Oct 2026)

Method note: direct fetches of play.google.com, apps.apple.com, appgoblin, exodus-privacy and psycatgames.com failed in this environment (DNS/proxy blocked). Store data below comes from search-engine snapshots of the listings (mostly the **US** storefront, `hl=en_US`) and from Apple developer pages. These snapshots are of uncertain date, so verify ratings live before relying on them. DE/AT-specific USK values only surfaced for a few apps.

## 1. Content ratings on Google Play (IARC) and the Apple App Store

### Takeaway
Real drinking-game apps sit at the top of both rating scales. On Google Play that is "Mature 17+" (US/ESRB) or "USK 18+" (DE) with "Use of Alcohol" plus language and sexual descriptors. On the App Store it is almost always 18+ under Apple's 2025 scale. Party apps without alcohol (Heads Up!, Psych!, Splash Impostor) sit at 12+/13+. Apple's scale changed in July 2025: 12+/17+ became 13+/16+/18+, and frequent alcohol references now map to 18+.

### Inferences
- For a card app whose core loop is "drink if…", expect **Apple 18+** (frequent alcohol references) and **USK 18 / PEGI 18 / ESRB-IARC Mature 17+** on Play. Some apps reach USK 16 or Apple 16+ (Vanilla King's Cup), probably through lighter questionnaire answers. Apps rated "Everyone" despite "Drink" in the title (Cyber Citizen) look mis-rated, not compliant.
- The Play rating shown depends on the viewer's region: the same app (Picolo) shows "Mature 17+" in the US and "USK 18+" in Germany. Our app's 17+ claim in AGENTS.md matches the US/IARC label. In DE/AT the label would be USK 18 (or PEGI 18 in AT, which uses PEGI on Play), not "17+".

## 2. Self-description: "drinking game" vs euphemisms; soft/no-alcohol modes

### Takeaway
There is a clear split. **On Google Play** many apps still put "drinking game"/"Trinkspiel" in the title (Picolo drinking game, Saufiii – Das Trinkspiel, Partybus · Trinkspiele). **On iOS** the market leader Picolo is titled "Picolo · Party game", and Funzy uses "Party Game" as its frame. The most popular new party app (Splash Impostor) explicitly says it is *not* a drinking game. The iOS rebranding fits Apple's guideline 4.3(b), which names drinking games as low-value spam.

### Cited Findings
- Picolo uses different titles per store: "Picolo drinking game" / "Picolo Trinkspiel" on Google Play and "Picolo drinking game" on Amazon (Picolo Inc.), but "Picolo · Party game" on iOS (Marmelapp). The iOS listing "describes itself as a party game rather than a drinking game". No source documents why the title changed. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.picolo.android&hl=en_US); [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Picolo-Inc-drinking-game/dp/B013X89KNI); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/picolo-party-game/id1001473964)
- In 2020 a developer noted that "iPuke: The Drinking Game, Picolo drinking game and Drink Roulette: Drinking Games" carried "Drinking Game" in their iOS titles. Picolo's iOS title has since changed to "Party game". — [Apple Dev Forums thread 649488](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/649488)
- **Splash – Impostor Game (Cranberry Apps)** states the app "is not intended for use as a drinking game and contains no alcohol-related content". It reports 4.3M+ installs and is monetized by a weekly "Splash Plus" subscription. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6744290388); [apppricinglab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/6744290388)
- Do-or-drink apps vary in naming. "Do Or Drink – **Party Games**" (Cyber Citizen) and "Funzy: Do or Drink **Party Game**" use the party frame, while KDR's "Do or Drink – **Drinking Game**" keeps the drinking frame. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cyber_citizen.do_or_drink&hl=en_US); [App Store Funzy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/funzy-do-or-drink-party-game/id1635869745); [Play KDR](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=dh3games.drinkinggame&hl=en_US)
- Vanilla's King's Cup is "King's Cup" on Play, while a competitor uses "King's Cup – Drinking Game" on iOS. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.vanilla.kingscup&hl=en_US); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kings-cup-drinking-game/id1367823394)
- German Play listings openly use "Trinkspiel/Trinkspiele" in their titles, and Hausparty states it targets "Personen ab 18". — see Play links in §1
- Apple guideline 2.3.8 requires that metadata (icons, screenshots, previews) "adhere to a 4+ age rating even if your app is rated higher". — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- On iOS, a "party game" title with alcohol only as an optional penalty is the de facto pattern that gets approved. Leading with "drinking game" in iOS metadata invites a 1.4.3/4.3 rejection. On Play, "drinking game" in titles is common and tolerated as long as the rating is 17+/18.
- Screenshots should be 4+-safe on iOS (no alcohol imagery, no explicit card text).

### Gaps
- I could not verify from live listings which apps offer an explicit "no alcohol"/"soft"/"family" mode. Snippets did not show it, apart from Picolo's free base vs. themed packs and the 18+-only "Hardcore" mode noted in §3.
- No screenshots were inspected.

## 3. Gating of spicy/NSFW/"hot" content

### Takeaway
The dominant model is **freemium with spicy packs behind IAP or (often weekly) subscriptions**. Age gating is mostly delegated to the store rating; in-app 18+ confirmations exist but are less visible. No separate "adult" app was found from the major players, though many standalone "dirty" apps exist as separate listings.

### Cited Findings
- **Picolo** is free with IAP. A subscription unlocks "premium features and unlimited content", and themed packs such as "Wild Night", "Caliente" and "War" sit behind it. — [mechanicsofmagic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2023/04/27/critical-play-picolo); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1001473964); [boomitparty cancel guide](https://www.boomitparty.com/blogs/how-to-cancel-picolo-subscription)
- **Funzy** sells a "Premium Subscription £7.99" (UK) for unlimited content, and a reviewer complained about "$5 per WEEK". — [App Store UK](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/funzy-do-or-drink-party-game/id1635869745)
- **KDR Do or Drink:** a reviewer cites "$3.99 a week / $20 a month". **Rayan Aboul Hosn Do or Drink:** reviewers say decks re-lock after the app is closed. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=dh3games.drinkinggame&hl=en_US); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/do-or-drink-drinking-game/id1457844294)
- **"I Have Never: Dirty Party"** offers a 3-day trial, then a weekly subscription is mandatory with no free tier. **"I Have Never: Dirty Adult"** cannot be played without a subscription or trial. **INI Never Have I Ever** sells per-deck IAP plus "Unlock All Decks" for $4.99. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/never-have-i-ever-18/id1481000967); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/never-have-i-ever-game-adults/id1210440961); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/never-have-i-ever/id917718075)
- **"Never have i ever drink game" (Play)** is 17+ overall, with its "Hardcore" mode restricted to 18+ in-app. — [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=never.have.i.ever.drink.game&hl=en_US)
- **"Wer würde eher ► Trinkspiel 18" (DE)** puts dirty cards behind a subscription from $2.99/week. — [App Store DE](https://apps.apple.com/DE/app/id1469183094)
- **Ad-funded tier:** remove-ads IAPs appear in King's Cup (Toscano) at $1.99, Circle of Death at $3.99 and Truth or Dare – Drinking at $0.99. Exodus static analysis of **Picolo 2.1.8** (report updated July 2024) found AppsFlyer, Facebook Analytics/Login/Share, Flurry, Google AdMob, Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics. Static detection does not prove the trackers are active. — [Exodus report](https://reports.exodus-privacy.eu.org/en/reports/191555)
- Apple guidelines 1.2.1(a) and 4.7.5 require age-restriction mechanisms only for creator/mini-app content that exceeds the app's rating. Otherwise the app's own rating covers its content. — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- A 100% free, no-ads, no-tracking app with spiciness as a slider (no paywall) is unusual. It contrasts sharply with the competitor norm of weekly subscriptions and AdMob/AppsFlyer SDKs, and this is a credible differentiator, including on Data safety and privacy labels.
- Because spicy content ships in the same app and is unlocked for free, the store rating must reflect the maximum content, i.e. 18+/USK 18. A spiciness slider does not lower the rating.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether Picolo or the others show an in-app "I am 18+" confirmation screen.
- Current subscription prices are from review snippets of varying dates.

## 4. Dynamic (server) content vs. shipped content; web versions

### Takeaway
There is little direct evidence. Picolo's Android build requests full network access and embeds multiple analytics/ads SDKs, which suggests online dependence. No source confirmed server-delivered card decks for any app. Web versions exist mainly for simple single-game sites (e.g. Psych Cat Games' "Never Have I Ever" online), not for Picolo/Funzy.

### Cited Findings
- Picolo 2.1.8 permissions include "full network access" and "run at startup", and its trackers are listed in §3. An older 1.24.2 report dates from 2018. — [Exodus 191555](https://reports.exodus-privacy.eu.org/en/reports/191555); [Exodus 11737](https://reports.exodus-privacy.eu.org/en/reports/11737)
- Psych Cat Games runs a browser version of "Never Have I Ever" at psycatgames.com/app/never-have-i-ever/. Its contents and gating could not be fetched. — [psycatgames](https://psycatgames.com/app/never-have-i-ever/); [psycatgames game page](https://psycatgames.com/games/never-have-i-ever/)

### Inferences
- Subscription-based apps very likely validate entitlements online. Whether the card text itself is remote is unknown. Shipping content in the binary, as our app does, means reviewers see everything, so the rating must match the spiciest card.

## 5. Removals, rejections, re-ratings and store-policy friction

### Takeaway
Apple is the hard gate. Guideline **4.3(b) explicitly lists "drinking games" among low-value app types**, and repeated submissions "may lead to removal from the Apple Developer Program". Guideline **1.4.3** bans apps that encourage "excessive amounts of alcohol". Developers report rejected new apps and blocked updates (e.g. Tomanji, "Drink it"), while legacy apps like Picolo stay listed. Google Play has no drinking-game ban but forbids encouraging "inappropriate" alcohol use and the "favorable portrayal of excessive, binge or competition drinking".

### Cited Findings
- **Apple 1.4.3:** "Apps that encourage consumption of … excessive amounts of alcohol are not permitted. Apps that encourage minors to consume any of these substances will be rejected." — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 4.3(b):** "Other kinds of apps, such as drinking games, Kama Sutra, fart, and burp apps, are mediocre, low-quality, or low-effort and do not add value to the App Store. Repeated submissions of this kind may lead to removal from the Apple Developer Program." — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 2.3.6:** developers must answer the age-rating questions honestly. Mis-rating "could trigger an inquiry from government regulators", and developers are responsible for local rating/warning requirements. — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Developer reports, Sep 2020–Mar 2021** (anecdotal, not official):
  - A developer of "Drink it – Drinking Game" was told by an Apple employee that "with the updated guidelines, drinking games are not permitted anymore… these apps can't be updated anymore and might get removed".
  - The **Tomanji** developer had "8 updates rejected" on iOS.
  - No official Apple reply appears in the thread. — [Apple Dev Forums 649488](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/649488)
- **Developer reports in other threads:** one party game was rejected because it "prominently features functionality that facilitates games encouraging users to drink" and was classified as a drinking-game app. Adding a non-drinking mode did not resolve it. — [Apple Dev Forums 688592](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/688592); [guideline update notes](https://cur.at/tol6uU?m=web)
- **Google Play, Inappropriate Content > Tobacco and Alcohol:**
  - Apps may not "encourage the illegal or inappropriate use of alcohol, tobacco, or nicotine".
  - Depicting or encouraging alcohol use by minors is banned, as is the "favorable portrayal of excessive, binge or competition drinking".
  - Introduced in 2019 with the wording "irresponsible use", later changed to "inappropriate". — [Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9878810); [Android Central 2019](https://androidcentral.com/play-store-adds-new-rules-sexual-content-hate-speech-and-marijuana); [Gummicube](https://www.gummicube.com/blog/google-play-restricts-apps-for-marijuana-alcohol-and-tobacco/)
- **Apple's mass re-rating:** in July 2025 Apple re-rated every app to the new 13+/16+/18+ scale, with a questionnaire deadline of 31 Jan 2026 for updates. — [iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/97995/apple-overhauls-app-store-age-ratings-with-new-13-16-and-18-categories)

### Inferences
- **For a new iOS submission:** positioning matters. Present the app as a multi-mode party game (minigames, votes, truth or dare, taboo) where drinking is an optional penalty. Keep "drinking game" out of the name, subtitle and screenshots, add a responsible-drinking note or alcohol-free play, and answer the questionnaire with frequent alcohol references (18+). This minimizes 1.4.3/4.3 risk but does not remove it.
- **On Google Play** the main risk is card text that glorifies binge or competitive drinking (e.g. "chug", "drink until…"). An audit of such cards is advisable.

### Gaps
- I found no documented removals of a specific major app (Picolo is still live). Evidence of Apple rejections is anecdotal and from 2020–21, apart from the guideline text itself.
- I found no reports of Google Play removing or re-rating drinking-game apps.
