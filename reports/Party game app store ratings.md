# Split Party Penguin into honest editions

Drinking-card apps on both stores sit at the top of every rating scale, and no in-app slider, age gate or downloaded pack changes that. On Google Play, Picolo, King's Cup, Do or Drink and the German "Trinkspiel" apps show **Mature 17+ in the US and USK 18+ in Germany**, and on the App Store they are almost all **18+** under Apple's 2025 scale. Party apps without alcohol, such as Heads Up! and Psych!, sit at **12+/13+**. Both stores require the rating to cover the most extreme content a user can reach. Shipping spicy cards as JSON from a website therefore does not lower the rating. It also breaks Party Penguin's offline and no-network promise and makes the app look like it is hiding content from review (Apple 2.3.1). The workable plan has three parts. First, build a **genuinely alcohol-free, low-spice store edition** from the same codebase, rated about PEGI/USK 12 and Apple 13+, with the drinking and spicy cards removed from the bundle at build time rather than hidden. Second, either keep the existing full game as an honestly rated **18+ Play listing** or move it to an **18+ PWA**: this needs a JMStV age label for German visitors, an Impressum and a click-through gate. Third, on iOS, avoid the "drinking game" category entirely, because Apple's June 2026 guideline 4.3(b) calls drinking games "mediocre, low-quality" apps and threatens developer-account removal for repeated submissions. Ice-cube penalties are fine for teens and adults if the card text says hold, never chew, and limits the time.

## Every real drinking app lands at 17+/18+; only alcohol-free party games rate 12–13

Competitor ratings are remarkably consistent once you separate games that tell people to drink from games that don't. The table below uses search snapshots of US listings unless noted. The dates of these snapshots are uncertain, so check the live stores before quoting them.

| App | Google Play | App Store | Monetisation of extra/spicy content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Picolo (Marmelapp) | Mature 17+ (US), **USK 18** (DE) | **18+**, titled "Party game" | Subscription; "Wild Night", "Caliente" packs |
| King's Cup (Vanilla) | Mature 17+ | 16+ | — |
| Do or Drink (KDR) | Mature 17+ | — | ~$3.99/week reported |
| Funzy: Do or Drink Party Game | — | 18+ | Premium subscription |
| Partybus · Trinkspiele / Drinking Game Collection | **USK 18** | — | IAP |
| Truth-or-Drink / Never Have I Ever "dirty" apps | 17+ | 18+ (one at 16+) | Weekly/annual subscriptions, unlock-all IAP |
| Heads Up!, Psych! (no alcohol) | — | 12+ / **13+** | — |
| Splash – Impostor Game | — | — | Weekly subscription, explicitly "not a drinking game" |

([Common Sense](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/psych-outwit-your-friends)); Splash ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6744290388)).

There are a few outliers. A Picolo knock-off with a clean name shows **USK 12** ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.Wheel.PartyPicolo&hl=en_GB)), and Cyber Citizen's "Do Or Drink – Party Games" is rated **Everyone** ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cyber_citizen.do_or_drink&hl=en_US)). These look like questionnaire answers that understate the content, not a model to copy. Google warns that "misrepresentation of your app's content may result in removal or suspension". It also says IARC authorities "may change your app's rating after a review" and can even refuse classification in their territory ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859655)).

Two things stand out in the business model. First, **the industry gates spicy content with money, not with age checks**. Picolo, Funzy, KDR and the "dirty" Never Have I Ever apps lock their hotter packs behind IAP or weekly subscriptions ([mechanicsofmagic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2023/04/27/critical-play-picolo); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/never-have-i-ever-18/id1481000967)). The age gating is left to the store rating, with at most an in-app 18+ toggle such as the "Hardcore" mode in one Play app ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=never.have.i.ever.drink.game&hl=en_US)). Second, the market leader carries a tracking stack: Exodus found AdMob, AppsFlyer, Facebook and Flurry SDKs in Picolo 2.1.8 ([Exodus](https://reports.exodus-privacy.eu.org/en/reports/191555)). Party Penguin is free, has no ads and never touches the network, which is a real and rare differentiator. Its Data safety section and Apple privacy label will both be empty, and every edition plan below should protect that.

The research found **no evidence that any major app downloads card text from a server** or ships a web version with different content. Picolo needs network access, but that is explained by its SDKs and subscription checks. Psych Cat Games runs a browser version of Never Have I Ever, but its contents could not be inspected ([psycatgames](https://psycatgames.com/app/never-have-i-ever/)). The "load spicy cards from a website" idea therefore has no proven precedent to follow.

## Store rules punish drinking positioning on iOS and binge framing on Play

**Apple is the hard gate.** Guideline 1.4.3 bans apps that "encourage consumption of … excessive amounts of alcohol" and rejects apps that "encourage minors to consume" it. The June 2026 revision of 4.3(b) goes further: "drinking games, Kama Sutra, fart, and burp apps are mediocre, low-quality, or low-effort and do not add value to the App Store. Repeated submissions of this kind may lead to removal from the Apple Developer Program" ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/09/app-store-guidelines-low-quality-apps/)). Reviewers decide by function, not by name. One rejection said an app "prominently features functionality that facilitates games that encourage users to drink, so it is classified as a drinking game app", and adding a non-drinking mode did not fix it ([Apple Dev Forums 688592](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/688592)). Developers of Tomanji and "Drink it" reported blocked updates, while Picolo, which was listed before the rule, stays live under the title "Picolo · Party game" ([Apple Dev Forums 649488](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/649488)). Apple's July 2025 scale maps **frequent alcohol references and frequent sexual content to 18+**. Infrequent alcohol or sexual content maps to 13+, frequent "mature or suggestive themes" to 16+, and frequent crude humour to 13+ ([Apple age ratings](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions)). Metadata must stay 4+-appropriate even for an 18+ app (2.3.8). Hidden or undocumented features are banned (2.3.1), and downloaded code that changes functionality is banned (2.5.2).

**Google Play tolerates drinking games but not glamorised excess.** It bans apps that "encourage the illegal or inappropriate use of alcohol". It also bans "favourable portrayal of excessive, binge or competition drinking" and any depiction of alcohol use by minors. Separately, it bans "explicit text, or adult/sexual keywords in the store listing or in-app", whatever the app's rating ([Inappropriate Content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9878810?hl=en-GB)). "Trinkspiel" in a title is common on Play ([Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.drinkaholic.app&hl=en_US)). The exposure comes from card wording that makes drinking a contest or demands big quantities. Party Penguin has about 46 card lines mentioning drinking or sips and one "takes a shot" Christmas card (`xmas_3`). It has no "chug" cards, so a quick audit for anything that rewards heavy drinking is enough. If the target audience includes children, the Families policy applies, and it explicitly forbids apps that "glamorize the use of alcohol". Google also judges audience from "imagery and terminology", so a cute penguin mascot on an alcohol app is a mild review risk ([Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335?hl=en); [Target audience](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)).

**Ratings must cover everything reachable, downloaded content included.** Google requires a new questionnaire whenever "changes to your app content or features … affect the responses" ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859655)). Apple requires honest answers and warns that mis-rating "could trigger an inquiry from government regulators" (2.3.6). Neither store lets an in-app age gate lower the store rating. Apple only requires a declared-age gate *on top of* the rating, and only for creator content and mini-apps (1.2.1(a), 4.7.5). Party Penguin's existing disclaimer gate is good practice, but it does not support a lower rating.

**The rating authorities point the same way.** PEGI's "Drugs" descriptor, which covers alcohol, only appears at **PEGI 16 or 18** ([PEGI – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEGI)). USK triggers its "Alkohol" descriptor when alcohol is shown "positiv oder häufig" ([USK](https://usk.de/die-usk-alterskennzeichen/)), although USK 12 can include hints of alcohol use and vulgar language. The current app's self-label of "17+" is the US/IARC label. In Austria and Germany the store will show roughly **PEGI 18 / USK 18**, so the docs and the store copy should say 18+.

## Downloading spicy JSON is the worst of the available options

Fetching spicy cards from a website after install keeps the full rating problem and adds new ones. The store rating must still reflect the downloaded content ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859655)). A 13+ app that unlocks sexual content from a server is exactly the misrepresentation that leads to removal or an IARC re-rating. On iOS, content that review cannot see invites a 2.3.1 "hidden features" rejection, and remote content that changes app behaviour moves toward 2.5.2/4.7 territory. Apple's guidelines name a "verified or declared age" gate as the minimum for over-rated remote content (4.7.5). The fetch also changes the product. Party Penguin promises **no network calls**, and adding a fetch means Data safety and App Privacy declarations, a server with an Impressum, uptime and a web origin, and the `INTERNET` permission in the Android manifest. It also creates a privacy story to explain: IP addresses reach your server. Server delivery is a tool for shipping new content without an update. It is not a rating loophole, and no competitor was found using it as one.

The honest alternative already exists in the architecture. Cards are plain data in `challenges.js` plus locale JSON, and `getDeck()` filters by pack and spiciness. A **build-time edition flag** can strip whole packs and spiciness levels from the bundle so that the binary simply contains no alcohol or spicy text. One example is a Vite `mode` or `define` such as `__EDITION__ = 'teen' | 'adult'`. Reviewers then see exactly what users get, and the existing unit tests can enforce it per edition.

## A 13+ edition works only if it never says "drink" or "sip"

The rating systems judge content, so swapping drinks for neutral penalties is the only coherent way to a lower rating. It has to be complete, though. Any surviving "drink", "sip", "cheers" or "shot" re-triggers the alcohol descriptor in the IARC questionnaire ([USK IARC page](https://usk.de/en/home/age-classification-for-games-and-apps/games-and-apps-in-the-iarc-system/); [Wikipedia, PEGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEGI)). No PEGI, USK or IARC text explicitly promises a lower rating for substitutes. The expected **PEGI 12 / USK 12 / ESRB Teen / Apple 13+** outcome is an inference from the criteria, backed by the comparable 12+/13+ ratings of Heads Up! and Psych!. For Party Penguin this means rewriting the strings as well as the cards. The disclaimer ("legal drinking age", "drink responsibly", "Never drink and drive"), the `result_drink` "DRINK! 🍺" label, the `mode_sips` mode, Medusa's "cheers!" and the word lists all need teen versions in all 10 languages. Spiciness needs a hard cap at the build level. "Infrequent sexual content" already lands at Apple 13+ and frequent suggestive themes at 16+ ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions)), so keep the teen edition to roughly levels 0–2 and audit those levels for innuendo. Truth-or-dare prompts written for drunk adults are the likeliest leak.

The penalty system is already there. Cards carry `points`, and swipe-left adds them to a scoreboard. Points, forfeits, dares, "swap seats", "speak in an accent for a round" and ice cubes can replace drinks without new mechanics. In Play Console, declare a target audience of **13–15, 16–17 and 18+, with no under-13 band**. That avoids the full Families policy, but it makes the "risky behaviour" guidance for 13–15 directly relevant ([Target audience](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159?hl=en)). Keep the drinking edition's 18+ audience separate.

**Ice cubes are safe enough if the wording rules out chewing.** Dentists consistently warn that chewing ice chips or cracks enamel, loosens fillings and crowns, and dislodges braces brackets, and braces are common at 13 ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/mind-body/chewing-ice-bad-for-teeth); [Colgate](https://testredirects.colgate.com/chewing-ice-teeth-damage-risks-revealed)). Choking guidance about ice cubes concerns children under about 4 ([mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/doc/preventing-choking-in-our-children-0/download)), and no source raised choking concerns for teens or adults. Write cards as "hold an ice cube in your hand until the next round" or "ice cube down your back", never "eat" or "chew". Cap holding times at seconds or one round, not "until it melts", because cold injury from prolonged skin contact was not researched. Keep "skip" available on every card. On Apple the ice cube also carries a hidden benefit: it removes the drinking-game classification that triggers 4.3(b).

## Ship the full game as an 18+ Play listing or a PWA, not inside the teen app

The adult content has three honest homes, and they can coexist. Each is built from the same repository with a different edition flag.

| Edition | Channel | Content | Expected rating / duty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teen | Google Play (+ App Store) | No alcohol words, spice ≤ ~2, ice/points penalties | ~PEGI 12 / USK 12 / Apple 13+; audience 13+ |
| Adult (store) | Separate Google Play listing | Current game, spice 0–6, drinking | Mature 17+ / **USK 18 / PEGI 18**; audience 18+; listing without sexual keywords. Not recommended for iOS (4.3(b)) |
| Adult (web) | PWA on own domain | Current game | No store rules; Impressum; `age-de.xml` label 16/18 + meta tag; click-through 18+ gate |

A **separate 18+ Play listing** is the lowest-effort way to keep the existing audience. It is policy-compliant as long as the listing avoids sexual keywords and binge framing, and in the EEA Play itself can block minors from acquiring 18+ apps ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859655?hl=en)). Two listings may draw a duplicate-content look from reviewers, so make the listings clearly distinct ("Party Penguin" vs "Party Penguin 18+").

**The PWA is the natural home for the spiciest tiers.** The Vite app already runs offline from `localStorage`, so a service worker and the existing manifest are most of the work. The law is light for a free, non-commercial Austrian provider. Austrian youth protection is a Länder matter aimed at minors drinking and at "jugendgefährdende" media such as glorified violence or pornography, and PEGI is binding only for the *commercial sale* of game media in Vienna and Carinthia ([oesterreich.gv.at](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/de/themen/onlinesicherheit_internet_und_neue_medien/internet_und_handy___sicher_durch_die_digitale_welt/4/Seite.1720600); [§ 10 WrJSchG](https://www.jusline.at/gesetz/wrjschg_2002/paragraf/10)). A game that references drinking is not alcohol advertising under the AVMSD, because there is no brand, product or payment involved ([European Audiovisual Observatory](https://www.obs.coe.int/en/web/observatoire/audiovisual-commercial-communications)). The DSA's platform duties do not reach a static site without user uploads, and they exempt micro-enterprises anyway ([DSA Art. 19](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-19/)). For German visitors, § 5 JMStV expects providers of 16+ content to use a protective measure. The cheapest recognised one is an **`age-de.xml` label in the server root plus a meta tag** ([JusProg](https://www.jugendschutzprogramm.de/anbieter/hintergrund/); [JMStV 2025](https://www.tlm.de/assets/uploads/general/DieTLM/Rechtsgrundlagen/JMStV_2025.pdf)). The Landesmedienanstalten doubt that a label alone is enough for 18+ content ([Medienanstalten 2022](https://www.die-medienanstalten.de/fileadmin/user_upload/die_medienanstalten/Service/Positionspapiere/20220620_Stellungnahme_Medienanstalten_JMStV.pdf)). Full age verification is mandatory only for "jugendgefährdend" content such as pornography, so keep the top spice level suggestive, not explicit. That also keeps the app inside Google's sexual-content rules. The EU country-of-origin principle likely limits German enforcement against an Austrian provider, but that point is general legal knowledge, not a finding verified in this research. Itch.io and F-Droid are secondary options. Itch.io deindexed adult NSFW content in July 2025 ([GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/07/game-store-itch-io-has-deindexed-adult-content-due-to-payment-processor-scrutiny/page=1/)). F-Droid excludes "explicit, age-restricted" apps and is reviewing its NSFW flag ([F-Droid blog](https://f-droid.org/en/2025/10/21/navigating-the-digital-markets-act-digital-services-act-and-the-online-safety-act.html)).

No case law was found holding a game maker liable for drinking-game harm. The known cases concern bars and hosts, and one beer-pong suit was dismissed because the player took part voluntarily ([Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2012/02/08/234657.htm)). The adult edition should still keep its safeguards: the disclaimer gate, "non-alcoholic drinks work too" wording, sips rather than shots (rewrite `xmas_3`), and a skip on every card.

## Conclusion

The real decision is which content lives in which binary, not how to word the rating questionnaire. Competitors use paywalls to manage spicy content and pay for it with weekly subscriptions and trackers. Party Penguin can manage it with build-time editions, and that keeps its main selling point: an app with nothing hidden and no network access. This approach also turns Apple from a near-certain 4.3(b) rejection into a plausible listing. A drinking-free teen edition is just a party game, and the drinking edition can stay where it is welcome, on Play at 18+ and on the web.

Three things remain uncertain. The ratings that IARC will actually return for the teen build (PEGI 12 vs. 16) are only knowable by running the questionnaire, so run it before investing in all 10 translations. The Austria/Germany legal points rest on search snippets of the statutes rather than consolidated texts. Google's announced developer-verification requirements for sideloaded Android apps were not researched, and they could affect any APK distributed outside Play. Rename the internal "17+" to "18+" today: it matches what DACH users already see in the store.
